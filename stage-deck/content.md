# Content

Read before writing any claim, heading, or body text. This file governs the words
themselves; arrangement is `archetypes.md`, drawing is `figures.md`.

---

## 1. The claim contract

A claim's job is **retrieval, then argument** — a reader skimming for one topic
must find it from the claim alone. Rhetoric that survives only in sequence fails
the deck's primary reading mode.

- **Subject + finding, in the source's own terms.** Every claim names what it is
  about and states what is claimed about it. A claim without its subject is a
  conclusion stripped of its address.
- **Front-load the noun a reader would scan for.** The search term appears in the
  claim's first few words, not after a rhetorical wind-up.
- **Use the source's terminology, never a synonym for variety.** A reader hunting
  a term the source uses must hit it verbatim.
- **No claim may need its body to decode.** If the claim reads as wordplay before
  the body and informative only after, invert it: state the finding plainly and
  let the body earn it.
- **Prefer the specific quantity, direction, or threshold** over an abstraction of
  it, whenever the beat has one — **but only where the count is the finding.**
  Taken claim by claim this rule is always right; read as a list, a dozen
  count-led claims become a drumbeat and the precision stops signifying. At the
  index test, cut the number from any claim where it is decoration and keep it
  where the count itself is what the beat proves.
- **Claims have a character budget, and it is a content constraint, not
  geometry:** ~55–62 characters at the standard claim size, ~42–46 at the
  statement size. A claim over budget wraps to a third line and breaks the
  vertical budget — so the budget binds at drafting time, including in the
  drafter session, not just at build.
- **The index test governs the whole deck:** the claims, listed bare in order, are
  the deck's table of contents. Every beat must be findable from that list alone,
  and the list must still carry the argument (the skim path). The verifier prints
  the list; read it as a reader would.

### Register shifts by role

- **Chapter titles name territory** — short noun phrases, no finding. Pith belongs
  here.
- **Body claims report findings** — the contract above, in full.
- **The opening and closing `statement` may carry the deck's thesis** in a more
  compressed voice — at most those two. A finding-beat wearing chapter-card voice
  is the failure this section exists to prevent.

## 2. Sub-heads and blocks

Sub-heads are claims one level down; the whole contract applies at that level.

- **Every block leads with its own assertion** — subject and finding, not a
  category label.
- **Parallel construction, and parallel in *kind*.** Items side by side take the
  same grammatical shape, land in the same length band, and are the same sort of
  thing. A condition, a shift, and a dependency are not three parallel items even
  if each is one line; two options and a caveat is two items and a footnote.
- **No block repeats another**, and no block re-lists what a figure beside it
  already labels.
- **The skim path survives alone:** claims and sub-heads read top to bottom carry
  the argument without the detail text.
- **One idea per block.** A block whose second sentence changes subject is two
  blocks.

## 3. Support lines, strips, and labels

- **A support line extends the claim; it never restates it.**
- **A strip item is a labelled fact: label and content, both present.** A row of
  labels with nothing beneath them is scaffolding without cargo — cut the strip or
  give each item its payload.
- **Labels in the meta register locate; text in the body registers asserts.** Do
  not promote a locator to an assertion by styling alone.

## 4. Basis notes

The basis is the stage's fine print: provenance, declared arrangements, derived
values, scope limits. It has a content contract, not just a length cap:

- **A basis note is required whenever the stage carries** a declared measurement
  arrangement, a derived or synthesised value, a schematic geometry, or a
  contested figure. Otherwise omit it.
- **It may qualify the body; it may never carry the body.** An argument demoted to
  the basis to make a beat fit is a coverage failure — the pressure-relief move is
  banned by name.
- ≤ 2 lines at full width — **mechanically enforced**: basis ink must stay inside
  its band, and a basis crossing the foot hairline is a build failure, not a
  style choice. Over two lines it is doing body work.
- **The declared pointer.** Between carrying an argument and omitting it there is
  a third register: one basis clause acknowledging a real cost or story the deck
  does not treat, placed at the beat where its subject appears. It is the
  coverage ledger's honesty made visible to the reader — use it for a material
  omission a careful reader would notice, and never as a gesture toward an
  argument the deck should either make or drop.

## 5. Voice

- Assertions in the present tense, active voice, no hedging the source does not
  hedge.
- **Never anthropomorphise the deck** — no "this slide shows," no "here we see."
  The stage shows; the text asserts.
- Numbers as numerals; units in the meta register's style; ranges with an en dash.
- Sentence case everywhere except the meta register's uppercase.
